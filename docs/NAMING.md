# Naming rules

Every name in Last Ink (people, places, factions, enemies, items, skills) follows this file. Issue #158.

**Why (Ojon, 2026-11-21):** we disclose that AI helped make the game, but names that *sound* AI-generated put
players off before they've played a minute. A name should sound like someone in the world gave it: a parent, a
parish clerk, a sergeant, a sailor, a cabaret manager. Not like an author reaching for "evocative".

Two goals, in this order:
1. **No AI tells** (section 1). Hard rules.
2. **Fits the Inklands** (sections 2 to 4). The world's themes (a paperwork empire, debt, erasure, gaudy decay)
   should come through in *how* people got their names, not by gluing ink words onto every one of them.

Decisions on existing names live in the review table (section 6). Ojon picks; a name Ojon likes stays even if
it breaks a rule here.

---

## 1. AI tells: never do these

### 1.1 Banned names
Names AI models reach for again and again. Never use them, or close spellings, for anyone or anything:

> Elara, Elora, Seraphina/Seraphine, Lyra, Lysandra/Lysandre, Kael, Kaelen, Thorne, Vex, Vesper, Nyx, Aria,
> Aurora/Aurore, Elowen, Isolde, Rowan, Wren, Sable, Raven, Silas, Corvin/Corvus, Caelum, Orion, Zephyr,
> Nova, Ember, Sylas, Aldric, Eldric, Cedric, Alaric, Theron, Lucian, Damien, Valerius, Mira, Mirabel, Liora,
> Celeste, Selene, Astra, Calla, Ophelia, Vivienne, Evander, Thalia, Briar, Oriel, Kestrel, Morrigan.

Plus any name ending in **-ara, -ael, -yra, -iel, -wyn** that isn't a real name in a real language.

### 1.2 Banned patterns
| Pattern | Example of the tell | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Theme-word compounds** everywhere: Ink-, Quill-, Ledger-, Lamp-, Wick-, Hollow-, Salt-, Velvet-, Gilt/Gilded-, Shadow-, Ash- + a noun | Inkmere, Quillpine, Ledgerside, Saltgrave, Hollow Marches | Real place-name grammar (section 3). At most **one** theme-word place name per region, kept for its landmark |
| **Nature-noun or object surnames** on everyone | Nightjar, Undermoss, Marrow, Quoin, Coil, Thornwood | Real surnames from trades, places, fathers, nicknames (section 2). Max **1 in 5** characters gets a surname that means something |
| **Virtue or abstract-word names** in bulk | Verity, Abeyance, Mercy, Solace | One per cast at most, and only when the irony is a story point |
| **"The Adjective Noun" / "The Noun That Verbs"** for places and bosses | The Drowned Light, The Tide That Forgot | A real name plus a plain description: "Pendower light (drowned)". Bosses may use titles, but vary the shape |
| **Fantasy spellings**: y for i, ae, doubled consonants, apostrophes, silent h | Lysandre, Velvette, Kae'lin | Spell it the way a clerk would write it down |
| **Rhyming or alliterating pairs** | Vane & Vesper, Hex & Tally | Siblings get ordinary sibling names (Cass and Kit Pruett) |
| **Every name equally fancy** | A whole cast of Ottolines and Jessamys | Mix: most people have common names (Tom, Agnes, Nell, Hob); a few have striking ones |
| **"X of the Y" epithets** on many characters | Ilse of the Mirror, Gerd of the Ashes | Rare. Real epithets are blunt nicknames: Big, Old, Ninefingers, Quarrel Jack |

### 1.3 Tests every new name must pass
1. **Say it aloud** in a sentence a mercenary would say: "Oi, ___, your round." If it sounds like a perfume, rethink.
2. **Search it.** If the top results are fantasy-name generators, AI fiction or another game's character, pick again.
3. **First letter and sound differ** from every cast member the player meets in the same chapter.
4. **Belongs to its region** (section 2). A Gutterwoods witch is not called Delphine.
5. **Not on the ban list** and doesn't use a banned pattern.
6. **Two syllables or fewer** for anything the player reads often (heroes, factions, big places). Long names are for
   pompous people, on purpose.

## 2. People: how the Inklands names them

The Great Ledger records everyone. That one fact explains most names in the world:

- **Registered names** are what's written in the Ledger: a given name plus a surname. Clerks wrote whatever they
  heard, so surnames are **ordinary, worn and often misspelled**: trades (Cooper, Fletcher, Tanner), places
  (Ashby, Thwaite, Pendower), fathers (Dickon, Hobson, Pask), and old nicknames (Pratt, Doolittle, Cripps).
- **Concord officials** use their **title first**, because the office matters more than the person: Assessor
  Penhallow, Toll-Sergeant Abernathy, Notary Fennick. Titles carry the bureaucratic theme so the names don't have
  to. Hyphenated job titles are the house style (Tithe-Captain, Clerk-Warden, Under-Registrar).
- **Struck-out people** (erased from the Ledger, deserters, debtors) lose their surname and go by a
  **nickname**: Ninefingers, Quarrel Jack, Big Corrie. This is where blunt, funny names belong.
- **Stage names** in the Causeway cabarets are *meant* to be glamorous, and everyone knows they're fake.
  A stage name always has a plain registered name behind it that someone uses to deflate it
  ("Saffron Kiss, née Maud Pickering").
- **Titles of respect** in the country: Mother, Old, Sister, Brother, Uncle, Granny. "Mother Gristle" and
  "Old Tobble" are the model.

### 2.1 Styles per region
Each region has a real-world source to draw from, so its names sound related to each other and different from the
next region. Use real names from that source; don't invent "in the style of".

| Region | Mood | Source for names | Given names (examples) | Surnames (examples) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Frayed Meadow, Hollowmark** | Rural market town, seedy | Plain English, Midlands and North, 1600s-1800s | Tom, Hob, Wat, Nell, Moll, Agnes, Ned, Bess, Kit, Joan | Pask, Tolley, Cripps, Hobday, Pratt, Ashby, Fennick, Sallow |
| **Gutterwoods** | Fungal, witches, poisoners | Old country dialect names and nicknames (Yorkshire, Lancashire, Welsh border) | Gristle, Mab, Hester, Bryn, Dorcas, Ezra, Huw, Ellis | Vane, Tibbs, Mossop, Pugh, Gethin |
| **Concord (everywhere)** | Paperwork, polite menace | English gentry and civil service, Latinate offices | Augustin, Marguerite, Ozias, Edmund, Constance, Humphrey | Penhallow, Thale, Abernathy, Pettigrew, Garrow, Hale |
| **Saint Ledger's Abbey** | Gothic, candlelit | Real saints' names from old Western calendars | Brother Anselm, Brother Odo, Sister Hild, Sister Werburgh, Abbot Wulfstan | none (monks give up surnames: they're struck from the Ledger on taking vows) |
| **Velvet Causeway** | Decadent bridge-city, masquerade | French and Venetian, 1700s; stage names in French | Margot, Hortense, Bérénice, Gaspard, Colombe, Anselme | de Saulx, Maltravers, Brissot; stage names: La Fauvette, Saffron Kiss |
| **Unfinished Coast** | Half-erased shore, fishing | Cornish and Breton | Jory, Tamsin, Kerra, Pascoe, Loveday, Morwenna, Yann | Trevail, Pendower, Hocking, Le Goff |
| **Gilded Spire** | Capital, gold and rot | High Concord: long, triple-barrelled, pompous on purpose | Aurelian Thale-Abernathy-Pound | |
| **The Margin** | Void | No names. Things there are redacted (black bars) or described by what's missing | | |

When two regions meet (a Causeway duelist working in Hollowmark), the person keeps their home region's name.

## 3. Places

Real place names are old, worn-down descriptions: "the ford by the ash trees" became Ashford. Build places the
same way, from ordinary English (or the region's language) elements:

- **Endings:** -ford, -ton, -by, -wick, -ley, -thorpe, -ham, -stow, -well, -bridge, -cross, -ings; Cornish
  Tre-/Pen-/Pol-; French -ville, -court only in the Causeway.
- **Descriptive names** from what's actually there: Old Mill Crossing, Gallows Ferry, Tithe Road. These are good.
- **Possessives** from a person: Hob's Wick, Abernathy's Cut.

### 3.1 The theme budget
Each region may have **one** place name that wears the world's theme openly (ink, ledger, quill, lamp, velvet,
gilt). That name goes to its landmark. Everything else uses ordinary place grammar.

### 3.2 Two names for one place
The Concord re-registers places with dull official names; locals keep the old one. Use this when a place needs a
thematic touch without a compound word: the map says "Tithe District Four", everyone calls it **Brack**. The
official name shows up in Concord dialogue and paperwork, which is funnier than inventing a third ink word.

## 4. Factions, enemies, items and skills

- **Factions** are named the way real groups are: by what they do, where they are, or who founded them
  (the Gutterwood Coven, the Free Companies, Pruett & Daughters). The Concord is the only grand name, because it
  named itself.
- **Enemies** get plain, descriptive common names that soldiers and farmers would use: Nibbler, Spitter,
  Letter-moth, Reedling, Tithe Enforcer. Keep it that way. Bosses may have titles (the Auditor, the Host).
- **Items and gear**: material + object, like a shop label (Boiled Leather, Brass Knuckles, Oilcloth Satchel).
  Avoid "of the ..." suffixes and invented materials unless the world has them (Gilt, White Ink).
- **Skills, techs and missions** use **idioms and accounting puns players already know**: Final Demand, Creative
  Accounting, Last Orders, Paid in Full, Chucking Out. This is the house style and the main place the theme lives.
  The test: it must be a real phrase. "Settle the Account" yes; "Inkstorm Cascade" no.

---

## 5. Process

1. New name needed: pick the region style (2.1), draft 3 options, run the tests (1.3).
2. Add it to the glossary used by localisation (#143) with pronunciation if not obvious.
3. Renames change **display text only**; internal ids (`"id": "quill"`) stay so saves keep working. If an id
   must change, add a save migration step and fixture (#110, `docs/SAVES.md`).
4. `tests/test_data.gd` `test_no_banned_names` fails on any name from 1.1 or any retired name (6.0) in data values
   or script strings. Add a name to `BANNED_NAMES` there when it's retired.
5. LLM *prose* tells in dialogue (as opposed to names) are covered by the script voice pass, #167.
